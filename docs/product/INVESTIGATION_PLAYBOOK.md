# Investigation Playbook

## Stage A — Identity
Confirm name, domain, location, phone where permitted, service area, and category. Disambiguate similarly named companies. Store matched identifiers and supporting URLs.

## Stage B — Customer journey probes
1. Discovery: relevant local service queries; note exact query, location, date, and result source.
2. Trust: public site details, service claims, testimonials; avoid equating marketing claims with performance.
3. Contact: visible phone, contact form, hours, emergency contact pathway; screenshot/DOM evidence where permitted.
4. Booking: presence/absence of a visible appointment request, accessible steps, obvious friction. Never complete fake bookings or submit real forms.
5. Follow-up: only owner-verified process or clearly attributable, contextual public comments; don't infer internal callback behavior.

## Stage C — Adaptive investigation
For each suspected gap, formulate a disconfirming question. Example: 'No online form found' → inspect header/footer/contact page and mobile nav; check if phone-first is deliberate. Stop when evidence is sufficient or costs exceed cap.

## Stage D — Opportunity card
- Observation (narrow, testable)
- Evidence IDs and URLs, captured_at
- Interpretation / possible customer impact (hypothesis)
- Alternative explanations
- Owner verification question
- Potential fix and agency capability match
- Confidence and limitations
- Recommended outreach angle, never assert unverified pain

## Prioritization
Rank by evidence strength, problem relevance, fixability, owner fit, and ability to verify; not an opaque 'AI opportunity score'. Keep separate fields rather than one magic number.

## Stopping rules
Stop on business identity ambiguity, restricted access, no actionable evidence, duplicate run, or cost/time budget. 'No finding' is a valid outcome.
