# PAIN FIRST — research and report doctrine

## The job
Discover costly or frustrating breakdowns in the path **need → discovery → consideration → contact → response → booking → follow-up**. Diagnose first; recommend a product last. A missing feature is not automatically a customer problem.

## Two evidence tracks
**Demand-side:** A customer asks for recommendations, reports no response, struggles to book, or abandons a confusing journey. Distinguish an individual anecdote from a pattern. **Supply-side:** An owner describes missed calls, scheduling errors, poor lead quality, unreturned messages, capacity problems, or failed automation. These tracks can corroborate each other but cannot be casually merged into a claim about a named company.

## Investigation sequence
1. Define company identity and service area. Verify website, phone, and relevant brand aliases; avoid confusing franchises and similarly named firms.
2. Write the **pain hypothesis** in customer language, e.g. 'A homeowner needing urgent service may not know how to request help after hours.'
3. Define falsification: 'If an obvious 24/7 contact path works, the hypothesis weakens.'
4. Collect source observations with URL, retrieval time, query/location, method, and source permissions. Save minimal permitted excerpts or metadata, not wholesale social copies.
5. Trace the actual journey using permitted non-destructive tests. Do not submit a booking, send a message, or make a call without approval.
6. Search for alternative explanations: seasonal capacity, emergency-only phone, separate booking domain, service-area limitations, owner already answering after hours.
7. Classify each item: OBSERVED / INFERRED / UNKNOWN / DISPROVEN. Attach evidence IDs to all observed statements.
8. Define a next verification step and potential intervention. The solution may be no automation at all.
9. Present a report an owner can challenge. Include source, date, scope, uncertainty, and what was not tested.
10. Draft optional outreach from the finding; human approval before sending.

## Signal → pain → verification → possible service
| Signal | Plausible pain (not fact) | Verification | Possible response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website lacks visible booking CTA on inspected pages | Customer friction | Check mobile/nav/alternate booking domain and owner process | Improve contact path |
| Public customer reports no callback | Follow-up inconsistency | Verify company identity, timing, owner workflow; never infer prevalence | Follow-up automation |
| Owner describes AI agent misbooking | Reliability and trust | Reproduce with authorized test scenarios | Evaluation suite and booking safeguards |
| Business absent in sampled AI search answers | Discoverability gap in sampled queries | Repeat by location/query/date, compare competitors | Content/structured data/local presence |
| Community members request HVAC referrals | Active local demand, not necessarily named-business failure | Verify geography, freshness, group rules, and genuine context | Relevant community participation |

## Owner-facing report contract
**Observed:** exact, scoped, source-linked facts. **Why it might matter:** a conditional customer-impact hypothesis. **What we don't know:** explicit limitations. **Recommended check:** simple next step. **Possible fix:** only after a gap is substantiated. No unsupported dollars, conversion claims, or 'guaranteed lost jobs'.

## Acceptance test
A skeptical owner can click every public evidence link (or see a permission-respecting description of a restricted source), identify which claims are hypotheses, and reasonably disagree without the report becoming misleading. If not, the report fails.

## Experiment to validate value
Start with 20 prospects; target at least five strong, manually reviewed findings; send five owner-approved tailored approaches; measure response, meeting, confirmed pain, and paid pilot separately. These are targets, not forecasts. Track minutes and provider costs per *validated* finding, not per scraped business.
